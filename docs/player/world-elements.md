# World elements

Some maps are more than walls and open ground. The ground itself can
burn you, slow you or heal you, and some things on the map strike on a
timer, open, move you or break. This page says what each one does to
you and how to tell it apart on the ground.

## Ground that hurts or helps

Stand in one of these patches and it works on you; its effect stays a
short while after you step out. Foes and allies are affected the same
way you are.

- **Scorched ground** sets you burning. Charred earth with glowing
  embers and an orange edge.
- **Lava** burns and cripples you. A dark crust split by flowing, glowing
  cracks that flicker.
- **Toxic fog** poisons you. Drifting acid-green puffs you can see
  through. Green on the ground always means poison.
- **Mire** slows you, and every attack or skill you use there costs you
  Energy. Brown mud with puddles and slow bubbles.
- **Foul water** lowers your Health regeneration. Murky dark teal water
  flecked with scum.
- **Tainted water** costs you Health for every skill you use in it. Dark
  olive water with an amber, oily sheen.
- **A draining spire** lowers your Energy regeneration near it. A stone
  obelisk with a glowing rune, standing in a violet wash with rings.
- **A spring** raises your Health regeneration. Clear blue water that
  sparkles and lights the ground around it.
- **A grove** makes you move faster. Grass tufts and small flowers.

## Hazards on a timer

These strike again and again at their spot, and hit whoever stands
there when they do.

- **A vent** throws up a column of flame that burns you. Between strikes
  it is a smouldering stone collar.
- **A gas spout** puffs out green gas that poisons you.
- **Rockfall** drops rocks that cripple you. Some fall in one place,
  others roll along a path and back.

Each one warns you just before it strikes: its edge ring swells in its
colour, and you also see sparks over a vent, wisps over a gas spout or a
darkening shadow under rockfall. Step out when you see it.

## Levers and gates

A **gate** is an oak door banded with iron. Shut, it blocks the way;
open, its doors swing aside onto a dark passage. A **lever** opens or
closes the gates it works: a stone base with a wooden arm, its brass
knob glowing when up and grey when down. Right click a lever to target
it, then press **Space** to walk there and throw it.

Some gates open from a **pressure plate** instead: a stone disc that
sinks and turns brass while someone stands on it. The gate closes when
the last one steps off, and its plate needs a few seconds before it
opens it again. Some plates answer only to one team.

## Portals

Walk into a **portal** and you appear at once at its exit. A portal only
goes one way. A working portal is a ring of rune stones round an aqua
swirl; one that is switched off is a dark dish with a dashed grey ring,
and a lever may switch it on. When you arrive, a white burst and a
spreading ring mark the moment, and the portal rests a few seconds
before it takes anyone again.

## Shimmering gates and cracked walls

A **shimmering gate** stops bodies, arrows and other projectiles, but
not sight: a spell cast straight at a target passes through it. It is a
shimmering aqua curtain between two stone posts with aqua lamps. When it
is open, only the posts and a faint dashed outline are left.

A **cracked wall** can be broken. It is warm, chiselled stone, unlike
the blue-grey walls that never break. Right click it, then attack it or
hit it with a damage skill. Its health bar shows while you point at it
or target it; cracks spread as it weakens, and it falls to rubble.

## Weather and atmosphere

Fog banks, smoke, steam, rain and snow are only for the eye. They never
hurt you, slow you or hide you. Fog is always pale and cool, never
green, so it cannot be mistaken for toxic fog.

## Try them

In the build editor's **Training Ground**, pick the **Wilds** scenario:
every kind of ground, hazard, gate and portal on this page is there,
with practice targets standing in the dangerous ones, so you can see
what each does before you meet it in a match.
