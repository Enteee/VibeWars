# Bots

A **bot** is a player the game plays for you. In a lobby you host, bots
play every place nobody takes, so you can practise, or play with fewer
friends.

## Who plays a bot

Nobody adds bots: a lobby lists its people only. When the
[Team Lobby](modes.md#team-lobby) ends, each side plays the Team Build it
chose, and every one of its characters nobody claimed is played by a bot,
under that character's name. A side with nobody on it plays a random
preset team, all bots. Someone who leaves during the Team Lobby leaves their
character to a bot.

In the lobby and the Team Lobby a bot looks like anyone else. In the
match it is tagged **BOT** on its nameplate, the target frame, the party
window and the match summary.

Bots are made for one match: once it is over, the lobby lists its people
again, and the next match's bots are made afresh.

## How bots fight

A bot plays the role its build gives it. One wielding a melee weapon is
**frontline**: it closes in and attacks its target. One with two or more
heals or protections it can cast on an ally is a **healer**: it stands
behind whichever ally is hurt most, away from the foes. When two or more
foes focus one ally, a healer puts its protection on that ally before the
hit lands. Every other bot is **ranged**: it keeps where every one of its
attacks reaches, and stands and fights a foe who comes too close. Ranged
bots and healers step to where fewer foes can reach them, and use pillars
as cover. A team turns on any foe in reach who drops below 60% health,
to finish them. In King of the Hill, while your team does not hold the
altar, the other team's Ghostly Hero on the altar comes before even that.
A bot that gets far ahead of its team waits for the rest before it pushes
on.

With no foe near, a bot throws a lever that opens a shorter way to the foes. Two bots also break down a wall that hides a much shorter way, striking it with their weapons.

Bot damage dealers hold their big hits, attack skills and damage spells,
and fire them together on the team's target, so the damage lands at once
rather than trickling in for a healer to undo: the slowest spell goes
first, a quicker one as much later as it is quicker. Meanwhile they keep
swinging and casting their hexes and conditions. A bot waits at most four
seconds for its teammates, and doesn't wait at all to finish a foe below
30% health. When the target is under a protective enchantment such as
Protective Spirit or Aegis, a bot with an enchantment strip, like Strip
Enchantment, takes it off first, and the others hold their hits for it.

A badly hurt bot that is losing its fight falls back behind its healer, or
to its base, and rests out of reach until it has most of its health back.

A bot resurrects a fallen ally only when the foes are not on top of the
body, more of its own team than of theirs standing near it, and never
while a foe close by is targeting the bot itself.

Bots side-step about half the skill shots aimed at them, such as a
Fireball or an attack skill's arrow, so stopping or turning works for
them as it does for you.

Bots walk round burning ground and other harmful patches when the way round
is not much longer, and step out of any they find themselves standing in.

A bot with an interrupt saves it for the casts that matter: a
resurrection or a Ghostly Hero claiming the altar first, then a heal or a
protection, an elite skill, or a big
hit such as an attack skill or a damage spell. It lets everything else,
and a plain weapon blow, go through. Like a player, it misses about one
in four of those casts.

A bot takes a human-like moment, a fraction of a second, to react to
what happens, so a quick interrupt or a late dodge can beat it.

In a three-team fight, bots fight one foe team at
a time: the team attacking them, or, while nobody is, the most hurt team
within reach. They stay on that team until another sets clearly more
players on them, and do not charge a fresh team while another one is
attacking them. A target their team called comes first, whichever team
it is on. Only players count: a team's ghost or guards standing nearby
do not make bots turn on that team.

## Random Arena

A Random Arena has no bots: every place is a real player, and you wait
until enough are queued.
